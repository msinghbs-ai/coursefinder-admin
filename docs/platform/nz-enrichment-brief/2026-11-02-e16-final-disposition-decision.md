StudySearch — Final E16 Disposition, Layer 4 Quarantine and Production Closure

Platform Admin direction — 2 November 2026

1. Executive decision

Do not delay the entire StudySearch project to manually correct 67 course records.

The platform already has Layer 4 review, evidence, quarantine, binding correction and exception-management mechanisms. Use those existing capabilities.

My direction is:

Contain incorrect data, park unresolved records in Layer 4, fix the underlying mechanism permanently, and continue production acceptance.

A record with no verified course link or fee is not inherently defective. Publishing an incorrect link or unsupported fee is defective.

Do not turn ordinary data-coverage gaps into release-blocking engineering tasks.

2. E16 — Immediate disposition

Use the existing E16 cohort of 77 records:

- 67 classified as wrong programme/year from title and URL evidence.
- 9 inconclusive.
- 1 known false positive.
- Up to 31 fees potentially originating from sibling-programme pages.

The existing classification is a candidate assessment, not proof that every record is incorrect. Preserve this distinction in the audit.

Required actions

1. Reconcile current course IDs, URLs, fees, evidence, locks and publication state.
2. Retain valid records unchanged.
3. Quarantine unsupported or demonstrably incorrect published course links through the existing governed hold mechanism.
4. Suppress unsupported fees where provenance verifies a wrong-course source, using approved reversible correction controls.
5. Send unresolved cases to the appropriate existing Layer 4 review kind, or retain a linked existing pending item.
6. Ensure each affected record has an explicit reason and a recovery path.
7. Do not generate duplicate Layer 4 items for the same issue.
8. Preserve course identity, existing authoritative data and history.

Do not manually search for and replace all 67 course URLs simply to obtain a zero-pending result.

No new acquisition or AI spending is required merely to park an unresolved value.

Use the current E16 approval and change-control boundaries; this mandate does not authorise destructive changes or unverified mass withdrawal.

Completion criterion

E16 is considered contained when:

- No confirmed wrong-programme destination is exposed to consumers.
- No confirmed wrong-programme fee is published.
- Unresolved records are visibly held with their evidence and reason.
- All holds have a route for later review.
- The original problem cannot recur through the corrected binding mechanism.

The number of parked records does not determine whether Gate B passes.

3. Correct the mechanism rather than the individual symptoms

Prioritise E16-1 and extend the existing binding validation where necessary.

The provider-course page matcher must verify:

- Exact provider identity.
- Course code or approved equivalent identifier.
- Qualification level.
- Programme name.
- Award and credit-point variant.
- Campus applicability where relevant.
- Domestic versus international audience.
- Handbook year or source validity.
- Programme-specific versus shared-provider information.

A matching domain or similar course title is insufficient.

For shared pages, validate the exact section or programme-specific evidence.

Where identity cannot be proved, refuse automatic binding and route the exception through existing Layer 4 controls.

Prevent downstream AI enrichment from reading a page with unsupported course identity.

Every binding rule must have regression fixtures for its previously documented failure classes, including genuine shared-page exceptions.

4. Ensure the platform can recover automatically

After the new binding validation is deployed:

- Re-evaluate eligible held records using existing stored evidence.
- Determine whether a valid page is already available.
- Automatically propose a corrected binding where evidence is sufficient.
- Admit only through an existing approved admission policy.
- Route uncertain cases to Layer 4.
- Acquire new evidence only where necessary and authorised.
- Prevent repeated processing of unchanged failures.

Do not bypass Layer 4 approval requirements merely to clear the queue.

The system should learn operationally through corrected rules and evidence reuse, not through ungoverned self-approval.

5. Mandatory housekeeping

Perform a controlled catalogue and pipeline housekeeping assessment covering:

- Duplicate active official links.
- Missing or conflicting primary links.
- Superseded pages still marked active.
- Wrong-year or wrong-programme bindings.
- Orphaned evidence references.
- Old source copies retained without clear lineage.
- Incorrect fees dependent on invalid bindings.
- Superseded review items.
- Stale or duplicate Layer 4 exceptions.
- Failed/retried work that is no longer actionable.
- Manual locks and protected values.
- Search projection discrepancies.
- Evidence-retention and storage lifecycle.

Classify every finding as:

- Correct automatically under an existing authorisation.
- Quarantine and route to Layer 4.
- Retain for audit/history.
- Retire under approved housekeeping.
- Require a specific Platform Admin decision.

Do not physically delete evidence or historical audit records as a shortcut.

Use existing housekeeping mechanisms, not a new clean-up subsystem.

9. Final operating principle

Data completeness is an ongoing operating responsibility. Data correctness and safe publication are release requirements.

A course without a verified URL is acceptable as an enrichment gap.

A course pointing to the wrong programme is not.

A course without a verified tuition fee is acceptable if the consumer correctly sees no supported fee.

A course publishing another programme's fee is not.

The permanent solution is evidence-backed binding validation and governed exception handling—not endless manual correction of individual records.

Proceed immediately with the existing E16 cohort, complete the permanent prevention mechanism, perform housekeeping and evidence auditing, and continue accelerated production acceptance.

Do not create another project phase or extend the deadline solely because Layer 4 contains unresolved data-enrichment items.
